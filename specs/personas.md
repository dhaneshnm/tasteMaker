# Personas — evidence base for story "Who" sections

Source: 24 negative App Store reviews of DailyArt (category leader), screenshots analyzed
2026-08-03 (`~/Desktop/bad_reviews`, IMG_9152–9179). Negative reviews only — these show
where the leader fails its users, i.e. where expectations are sharpest.

## 1. Daily Ritual Learner — CORE persona (~8 reviews)
Opens from the daily push or widget, reads the blurb in 1–3 minutes, closes. Art-curious,
not expert. Values calm and simplicity.
- "It's the only app I have that I allow notifications… the single, daily notification."
- "It doesn't try to steal all of my attention. A great way to massage the mind."
- "Art with tea… this is the first thing I open"
- Does the "extra-curricular" readings when offered; wants "to be schooled."
Serve with: baseline items 1–3. Guard the calm — no interruptions, no upsell noise.

## 2. Collector / Wishlist-keeper (~6 reviews — loudest pain)
Favorites are the product: a personal catalogue built over years. Often budget-constrained;
the collection is aspirational (prints to buy "when I have a better job").
- "All of my favorite pieces… collected over multiple years are out of reach… locked
  behind a paywall I cannot afford."
- "Half the reason I used it was to have a catalogue of great art."
- "The favorites feature should be free to all users."
Serve with: baseline item 4. **Favorites free forever, never data-hostaged.** Breaking this
is DailyArt's single most reputation-destroying move.

## 3. Educated Depth-seeker (~5 reviews)
Took art-history classes; reads critically; catches factual errors ("she is clearly sitting
in a chair," not standing); finds over-famous works "banal"; demands non-Western coverage
(India, China, Japan, Middle East, Vietnam named); compares app to Wikipedia.
- "The descriptions are informal and contain only a surface-level analysis."
- "Repeated tired old Van Goghs which are already super famous."
- "Almost a complete disregard for works outside of the western canon."
Serve with: editorial quality bar — accurate, specific, technical when it earns it, no mood
fluff, no promo cross-links inside blurbs. Mix beyond the Euro-canon greatest hits.

## 4. Reference Browser (~3 reviews)
Treats the app as a searchable museum: wants directory, artist discovery, specific works.
- "It's too hard to discover artists… should be set up like going to an in-person museum."
- "Most results on the search feature [are paywalled]… collections frustratingly incomplete."
- Compares to Google Arts & Culture (free).
NOT baseline. New-slot candidate at Phase 3 gate.

## 5. Aesthetic Personalizer (~1–2 reviews)
Wants art matching their taste/vibe: genre, era, color scheme; widget as home-screen decor.
- "Allow users to pick a favorite genre… or specific color scheme… all I get are paintings
  from before 1900AD and I just don't like them."
NOT baseline. New-slot candidate at Phase 3 gate.

## 6. Burned Loyalist (lifecycle state, ~7 reviews)
Multi-year users who paid once, evangelized, then the one-time-purchase → subscription
switch logged them out, broke purchase restore, paywalled favorites, added ads.
- "I paid for premium… the app still does not recognize me… so disappointed."
- "Insulting to your loyal users who've been with you for years."
Lesson, not target: never convert paid entitlements downward; never gate previously-free
retention features; restore must work.

## Outlier (n=1)
Ideological rant about a women-artists collection — not a target persona. One real gripe
inside it: blurbs formatted like promo newsletters (links/emoji top and bottom) cheapen the
editorial voice.

## Standing implications for Tastemaker
1. Baseline serves persona 1; persona 2 defines the favorites contract (free, permanent).
2. Persona 3 sets the hand-written-blurb bar and argues for range beyond Euro-canon hits.
3. Personas 4–5 are the strongest evidenced New-slot candidates — decide at Phase 3 gate.
4. DailyArt's negative reviews are ~monetization self-inflicted wounds; our free-at-launch
   baseline sidesteps all of them. Keep it that way until premium is a real spec.
