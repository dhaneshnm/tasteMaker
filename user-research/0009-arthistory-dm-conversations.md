## Synthesis — what four strangers agree the scarce goods are

Content is free and abundant (all four consume YT/books/museums). What the two paying /
certificate-pursuing respondents paid for, and what the beginners lack, is:

1. **Framework / structure** — where to start looking. The practitioner (DrMoneylove)
   confirmed the skill is trainable but had *no framework to transfer* — "no real
   secret." The skill exists; the scaffold doesn't. That gap is the product.
2. **Vocabulary as a seeing tool** — names sharpen perception ("having the names …
   made me see them more clearly"; "labels you attach to things you're already
   seeing"). Direct validation of the protocol's trained-eye delta + glossary mechanic.
3. **Schedule + accountability** — the Barnes class's value was that it *forces time*
   and has peers, more than its content. Maps to the daily-push habit moat: the push is
   the scheduled class, minus peers.
4. **Ceiling note:** "nothing replaces seeing art in person" (jrblockquote). App is
   practice between museum visits, not a museum substitute — consistent with the
   protocol's MFAH sits.

Caveats: n=4, self-selected (people who answer DMs about art practice), and the
blank-screen problem statement is the owner's own, not a stranger's unprompted words.
The strangers validate trainability, vocabulary-as-lens, and structure-as-scarce-good —
they do not yet independently voice the blank-screen problem.
