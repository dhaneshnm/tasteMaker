## 1. Method frameworks — the teachable structures

These are the pedagogies. The coach's prompt design will be some synthesis of these.

**Visual Thinking Strategies (VTS)** — Abigail Housen & Philip Yenawine (ex-MoMA education
director). The dominant museum-education method. Three questions, always the same, never
lecture-first: *"What's going on in this picture?" / "What do you see that makes you say
that?" / "What more can we find?"* Evidence-demand is the engine — every claim must point
at something visible. Book: Yenawine, *Visual Thinking Strategies* (2013).
**Study for:** question-first coaching; the evidence-pointing loop; how far a coach can get
with zero art-history knowledge required from the user. Its known weakness — VTS forbids
supplying context, which caps depth — is exactly where a hand-written editorial layer can
go further.

**Harvard Project Zero thinking routines** ✅ — free library, "helped millions of learners"
(verified in 0002). The relevant routines:
- *See / Think / Wonder* — observe → interpret → question. The canonical 3-step.
- *Zoom In* — reveal the artwork progressively, section by section, revising the story at
  each reveal. **This is a keyed-prompt app mechanic already designed, free, and proven in
  classrooms.**
- *Looking: Ten Times Two* — list 10 words about the work, then look again and list 10 more.
  Forces the second look, which is where noticing happens.
**Study for:** Zoom In as the closest existing blueprint for "look at this section, notice
X"; routine *sequencing*; how routines stay short.

**Slow looking** ✅ — Shari Tishman (Project Zero), *Slow Looking: The Art and Practice of
Learning Through Observation* (Routledge, 2017). Tate runs it as visitor programming
("A guide to slow looking," verified). Thesis: observation is a skill built by deliberately
decoupling looking from immediate interpretation; time itself is the instrument.
**Study for:** time-boxed looking prompts; the argument for *withholding* information as a
coaching move; language for making slowness feel like a feature, not a chore.

**Feldman method** — Edmund Feldman's 4-step art criticism model, standard in US art
education for 50+ years: *Describe → Analyze → Interpret → Judge.* Describe = inventory only
what's visibly there; Analyze = how elements relate (composition, color, light); Interpret =
what it means; Judge = deferred to last, on purpose.
**Study for:** the progression skeleton. A per-painting prompt set that walks D→A→I is the
most defensible curriculum structure available — boring name, bulletproof bones.

**Panofsky's three levels** — Erwin Panofsky's iconography model: pre-iconographic (what's
depicted — a man, a dog, a candle), iconographic (what it denotes — that candle is a vanitas
symbol), iconological (what it reveals about its world). The scholarly backbone behind every
"hidden symbol" explainer video.
**Study for:** the symbol-decoding layer — the single most audience-pleasing move in the
validated video register (Payne/Nerdwriter run on it); how to grade prompts from surface to
meaning.

**Amy Herman — The Art of Perception** ✅ — coached looking sold to the FBI, NYPD, Interpol
at $10–20K/engagement (verified). Uses artworks to train professional observation: describe
only what you see, separate perception from inference, articulate precisely. Books: *Visual
Intelligence* (2016, NYT bestseller), *Fixed.* (2021). ⚠️ Her exact staged framework —
verify from the book before citing its steps.
**Study for:** the perception-vs-inference discipline; proof language that "learning to look"
transfers beyond art (her whole pitch); prompts that work for a viewer with zero art
vocabulary.

**Barnes method** — Albert Barnes (Barnes Foundation, Philadelphia): formalist comparative
looking — light, line, color, space — with works hung in "ensembles" that force comparison,
famously without wall labels. Rooted in John Dewey's *Art as Experience*.
**Study for:** comparison as a coaching move (pair today's work with one prior favorite);
looking without labels as a deliberate constraint.

**Formal analysis (the elements)** ✅ — Smarthistory's "How to do visual (formal) analysis"
was in 0001's corpus (1.17M views). Line, shape, color, light, space, composition. The
vocabulary layer everything else stands on.
**Study for:** the vocabulary drip — which 20 terms an untrained viewer actually needs, in
what order.

## 2. Books — the canon of looking-pedagogy

**John Berger — *Ways of Seeing* (1972)** — book + free BBC series (4 × 30 min on YouTube).
The register model 0001 already named (Berger/Walsh lane). Demystifies rather than
mystifies; treats the viewer as intelligent; politics of images included.
**Study for:** editorial voice — how to talk up to a layman. The single most recommended
item in 0002's Reddit "how do I appreciate art" threads.

**Kenneth Clark — *Looking at Pictures* (1960)** — 16 paintings, one chapter each, Clark
narrating his own looking *process in stages*: first impression → scrutiny of parts →
historical knowledge brought in → renewed whole-picture seeing. He also states his attention
span for a picture is about as long as an orange takes to peel — then structures around that.
**Study for:** the phase structure of a single sustained look; honesty about attention
limits; the model for "one artwork, treated completely."

**Mary Acton — *Learning to Look at Paintings* (Oxford, 1997/2009)** — the standard
university primer; organized by formal problem (composition, space, form, tone, color)
with worked examples per painting.
**Study for:** curriculum sequencing; which formal concept to teach on which *kind* of
painting (don't teach color on a grisaille).

**Anne D'Alleva — *Look! Art History Fundamentals*** — the "how to do art history" study
guide; recommended organically in 0002's Quora findings. Compact treatment of formal
analysis + contextual analysis and how to write about looking.
**Study for:** the student-facing distillation of methods; phrasing of analysis questions.

**Shari Tishman — *Slow Looking* (2017)** ✅ — see §1.

**Philip Yenawine — *Visual Thinking Strategies* (2013)** — see §1.

**Amy Herman — *Visual Intelligence* (2016)** ✅ — see §1. Goodreads 2,721 ratings (verified).

**Ben Street — *How to Enjoy Art: A Guide for Everyone* (Yale UP, 2021)** — explicitly
anti-gatekeeping: you already have the skills; artworks as encounters, not exams.
**Study for:** tone calibration for the intimidated viewer — Priya's "hated feeling locked
out" feeling, addressed in prose.

**Ossian Ward — *Ways of Looking* (2014) / *Look Again* (2021)** — contemporary-art looking
method; ⚠️ built around his "TABULA" mnemonic (time, association, background, understanding,
look again, assessment) — verify the acronym expansion before quoting.
**Study for:** how to coach looking at work with no recognizable subject — where formal
analysis and symbol-hunting both fail; matters for curation range beyond the Euro-canon.

**Will Gompertz — *What Are You Looking At?* (2012)** and **Grayson Perry — *Playing to the
Gallery* (2014)** — the two most successful mass-market "art without intimidation" books.
**Study for:** humor as an access tool; what a bestselling register sounds like at book length.

**E.H. Gombrich — *The Story of Art* (1950)** — not a looking method, the context spine; the
other item Reddit recommends constantly (0002 §3.3).
**Study for:** how much context a blurb actually needs — Gombrich's gift is ruthless
selection, the same craft bar as the daily blurb.

**John Ruskin — *The Elements of Drawing* (1857)** — the historical root: drawing taught as
seeing ("the innocence of the eye"). Betty Edwards' *Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain*
(1979) is the modern mass-market descendant.
**Study for:** the deep tradition claim (coached looking is 170 years old, not a startup
idea); optional sketch-prompt mechanics if the coach ever includes "draw what you see."

## 3. Video — the validated register (what 8.35M + 15.4M views already watch)

All verified at scale across 0001 + 0002 corpora. The coach's *voice* competes with these.

- **Great Art Explained (James Payne)** ✅ — 15 min, one painting: hook → context → zoomed
  details → meaning → one-line landing. The register leader (1.9M subs). **Study for:** per-
  painting narrative arc; where he zooms and why; his rule of no filler.
- **Smarthistory (Beth Harris & Steven Zucker)** ✅ — two scholars in conversation *in front
  of* the work, pointing. Khan Academy's art-history partner. **Study for:** the pointing
  dialogue — closest video analog to keyed prompts; disagreement as engagement.
- **Nerdwriter1 — "The Death of Socrates: How To Read A Painting"** ✅ — 4.4M views; 0001's
  anchor. Single-work close read as visual essay. **Study for:** the title promise itself —
  "how to read a painting" is the job stated as content.
- **The Art Assignment (PBS, Sarah Urist Green)** ✅ — "The Case For…" series (Rothko episode
  in 0002 corpus) plus its earlier format: artists assign the viewer an *actual exercise*.
  The only major channel that ever converted watching into doing. **Study for:** assignment
  design — what a "do this now" prompt looks like in this domain.
- **Sister Wendy (BBC, 1990s)** — the most recommended video content in 0002's Reddit
  threads, decades after airing. Pure warmth + fearless specificity in front of the work.
  **Study for:** why she outlives every competitor: total absence of condescension.
- **Simon Schama — *The Power of Art* (BBC, 2006)** — one work per episode, treated as drama.
  **Study for:** stakes-setting — why this painting mattered *that day*.
- **John Berger — *Ways of Seeing* (BBC, 1972)** — see §2.
- **The School of Life — "What is Art for?"** ✅ (0002 corpus, 1M views) — art justified by
  function (de Botton's *Art as Therapy* frame). **Study for:** the "why bother" on-ramp for
  users who don't yet care.
- **TED-Ed one-offs** ✅ (Mona Lisa, 2.5M views in corpus) + **Curious Muse** ✅ — the
  short-form end. **Study for:** what survives compression to 5 minutes.

## 4. Courses — free and paid (the direct N5 competitors)

- **DailyArt — "How To Look At Art"** ✅ — the leader's **$0 lead magnet**: 6 lessons, 1h20m,
  by founder Zuzanna Stańska; 685 reviews, 4.81★ (verified; their *paid* courses sell at
  12–52-review volume). **Study first, lesson by lesson** — this is the exact free bar a
  paid coach must beat, from the exact competitor. Map: what do its 6 lessons cover, what
  register, where does passive video leave the learner unable to *do* anything?
- **MoMA on Coursera** ✅ — *Modern Art & Ideas* (487,909 enrolled), *Seeing Through
  Photographs* (420,838), *What Is Contemporary Art?* (169,681). Free to audit.
  **Study for:** how the institution structures weeks/units; the discussion prompts; scale
  proof of C1 (the job), not of payment.
- **The Great Courses — "How to Look at and Understand Great Art"** ✅ — Sharon Latchaw
  Hirsh, 36 lectures, continuously sold since 2011. **Study for:** the complete long-form
  syllabus — the fullest single curriculum map of this exact subject in existence. Steal the
  table of contents as a checklist of teachable concepts, then cut 90%.
- **Khan Academy / Smarthistory curriculum** ✅ — the whole art-history survey, free, 880+
  videos, 2,000+ essays. **Study for:** what "the whole curriculum for free" means
  concretely; anything the coach charges for must be something this *structurally can't do*.
- **Art Explora Academy** ✅ — free French nonprofit app/site found in 0002's sweep;
  learning paths + quizzes, certification validated by Sorbonne University; ~5 App Store
  ratings. **Study for:** a live gamified-art-history execution with institutional content
  and near-zero traction — a cautionary product-shape lesson, not a content one.

## 5. Audio — coaching without a screen

- **The Lonely Palette (Tamar Avishai)** — podcast; each episode opens with museum-goers
  *describing the work before it's named*, then a close read. **Study for:** the
  describe-before-reveal move — pure looking-first pedagogy in audio; also the warm-expert
  register at ~20 min.
- **⚠️ A Long Look (Karen Jackson)** — podcast built as *guided looking*: you're told to
  open the image and look along in real time. Verify current status. **Study for:** the
  only found format where the audience holds the artwork while being coached — the app's
  core loop, minus the app.
- **ArtCurious (Jennifer Dasal)** ✅ — recommended in 0002's Reddit threads; narrative art
  history ("the unexpected, slightly odd"). **Study for:** hook craft for the
  casually-curious.
- **Museum audio guides generally / Smartify tours** ✅ — the incumbent in-gallery coached
  experience. **Study for:** everything wrong with it (linear, one-pace, no response from
  the viewer) — the negative spec.

## 6. Interactive & digital executions — the mechanic references

The short list of things that already do some version of "look here, notice X" on a screen.

- **NYT "Close Read" interactives** ⚠️ (verify current URL set) — scroll-driven single-
  artwork essays: the artwork stays pinned, zooms and pans as annotated text advances
  (e.g., the Hokusai *Great Wave* close read). **The best digital execution of coached
  looking found anywhere.** Study for: scroll-as-pacing, zoom keyed to sentence, how little
  text sits on screen at once. This is the interaction-design bar.
- **NYT Learning Network — "What's Going On in This Picture?"** — weekly VTS feature since
  2012(ish): image with caption stripped, the three VTS questions, moderated student
  discussion. **Study for:** VTS running at internet scale with zero app.
- **Rijksmuseum — "Closer to Johannes Vermeer"** ⚠️ — 2023 online experience, Stephen Fry
  narration; guided gigapixel journeys into each painting. Verify availability. **Study
  for:** museum-grade guided zoom; pacing and script of a "walk into the painting."
- **Second Canvas (Madpixel)** ✅ — gigapixel audio-tours sold museum-by-museum (Prado app:
  7 ratings — verified). Mechanically closest product to keyed prompts; linear and passive.
  **Study for:** what gigapixel + narration feels like, and why it didn't travel as a
  consumer product.
- **Google Arts & Culture zoom stories** ✅ — editorial slideshows over gigapixel scans.
  **Study for:** the free baseline of "zoom + caption"; its flatness is the opening.
- **Project Zero "Zoom In" routine** ✅ — see §1; a reveal-mechanic spec in classroom form.
- **The 2025–26 app wave (Curator, Paintly, Connoisseur, ArtHist…)** ✅ — what they actually
  built is recognition quizzes, not looking coaching (0002 §3.5). **Study for:** the gap
  between "train your eye" copy and drill-the-flashcard reality — the execution space left
  open.

## 8. Synthesis — recurring moves across all sources

Read the library and the same seven moves keep appearing. Any Tondo coach is a selection
from this move-set, in the hand-written voice:

1. **Describe before interpreting.** Feldman, VTS, Herman, Lonely Palette — universal rule.
   First prompts must demand only eyes, zero knowledge.
2. **Evidence-pointing.** "What do you see that makes you say that?" — the VTS engine;
   keyed prompts are this question with the pointing pre-done.
3. **Progressive disclosure.** Project Zero's Zoom In, NYT Close Reads, Payne's structure —
   reveal in stages, let the story revise itself.
4. **The second look.** Ten Times Two, Clark's renewed seeing, slow looking — the outcome
   (0001's "I never noticed") lives in look #2, so the coach must engineer a return to the
   image, not a scroll past it.
5. **One work, treated completely.** Clark's chapters, Payne's episodes, Schama's episodes,
   Lonely Palette — never survey. Perfectly congruent with one-artwork-per-day.
6. **Vocabulary dripped, never front-loaded.** Acton/formal elements — one term per day,
   earned by the artwork that demonstrates it.
7. **Time as instrument.** Tishman, Slow Art Day — duration itself changes what's seen;
   a coach can time-box ("30 seconds on her hands") where video can't.

**The N5 conclusion, restated for the builder:** every *piece of information* above is free
somewhere. What none of the free material can do: key the coaching to *today's* artwork, in
the user's hand, at the user's pace, demanding a response, inside an existing daily habit,
in a voice that isn't AI slop. That delta — not the content — is the only thing a coached
layer can charge for, and it is exactly the moat set already named in `CLAUDE.md`
(hand-written voice + habit mechanics).
